The Price of Place

Five figures, one argument: Computer Science transfer preparation is income-gated in a way the rest of the curriculum is not — and the gate operates precisely where access is contestable.

Beat 1 · The hook

Nine programs, three fates

Share of a quartile's districts from which a complete transfer path formally exists, poorest quartile to richest, one row per UC Computer Science program.

Figure 1
Slope rows, grouped by regime

First thing the eye learns: two of the nine programs are shut to every district in California, one is open to almost all of them, and the other six are long arrows that all point the same way — toward money.

Three shapes, not three colours: a dot on zero, a short dot pair at the top, a long sweep. The regime is legible before a single label is read.

* Regime means are verified (closed pair 0%; Berkeley ≈89–96%; the remaining six 44% → 92%). Per-program endpoints shown here are illustrative, constrained to whole districts and to the verified regime means — swap in the real per-program shares before publication.

Beat 2 · The headliner

Two maps, nearly the same map

The 72 community college districts, twice. Left, coloured by the wealth of the area they serve. Right, coloured by how many of the nine CS programs they can formally reach. Same ramp, same places, different variable.

First thing the eye learns: the pale parts of California are the same pale parts on both maps — where the money isn't, the Computer Science isn't either.

One ordinal ramp used twice is what makes the coincidence readable. Two different palettes would have forced a comparison the eye can't make.

* Points are district service-area centroids, not boundaries: a filled district polygon would over-claim precision in the rural north, where service areas are enormous and thinly populated. Per-district values are illustrative and constrained to the verified quartile means; the geometry is real. The human denominator is the one number this beat still needs.

Beat 3 · The evidence

Same start, triple the response

Four quartile means. No fitted line, no model, no smoothing — the poorest quartile of districts against the richest, for Computer Science and for everything else the University of California teaches.

Figure 3
Two quartile paths, divergence shaded

First thing the eye learns: the two lines leave the poorest quartile together and arrive at the richest 29 points apart — the curriculum creeps, Computer Science leaps.

Bounded shares plus a fitted line overshot 100% in earlier drafts. Four means joined by straight segments cannot overshoot, and cannot be mistaken for a model.

* Field = 897 non-computing UC majors; ~34 computing-adjacent programs (computer engineering, informatics and similar) are excluded from both subject and field. Subject = exactly the nine registry Computer Science programs. Quartiles are of districts, not of programs: 940 programs share the same 72 income observations, and nothing here should be read as 940 independent tests. Q1 and Q4 values verified; Q2 and Q3 interior points are illustrative pending the quartile cut for the field series.

Beat 4 · The mechanism

The gate has a course catalogue

The courses that actually block a path, named. For each one: the share of poorest-quartile districts where it is missing and load-bearing, against the share of richest-quartile districts.

Figure 4
Named-course slope rows, two panels

First thing the eye learns: the Computer Science blockers are long bars that shrink with money; the field's blockers are dots that don't move — because nobody teaches them anywhere.

This is the quotable figure. A bar collapsing from 78% to 22% names a fixable absence: a discrete mathematics section, staffed.

* Binding-missing means the course sits inside a requirement group the engine reports as unsatisfied — spare alternatives in already-satisfied choice lists are not counted. Course names and shares here are illustrative of the exact ranked list, which the engine can produce; the cut we need is that ranking split by income quartile, for CS and for the field separately.

Beat 5 · The discipline

Two objections, answered before they're raised

Don't big majors just have more courses? And isn't a strong income response normal for a large major? Panel A answers the first by changing currency; panel B answers the second by plotting the whole comparison population instead of summarising it.

Figure 5
Currency swap + conditional scatter

First thing the eye learns: the story only exists in the honest currency — and even there, Computer Science is consistently above par rather than freakish.

Both panels are self-audits. The zoom frustum shows exactly which slice of the raw panel the binding panel magnifies, so the rescale can't read as a cherry-pick; the scatter shows the entire comparison population rather than asking the reader to trust a percentile.

* Binding endpoints verified (CS 3.15 → 0.96; field 3.46 → 2.39). Panel B's reference lines are binned medians and binned 95th percentiles, not fitted curves — bounded shares plus OLS overshot 100% in earlier drafts. Per-program effect distributions spike at zero because closed-everywhere and open-everywhere programs have no income response that could exist; that is structure, not noise, so those three programs sit in a labelled band rather than being dropped or plotted at a false zero. Every major is estimated over the same 72 districts: the cloud is 897 views of 72 income observations, not 897 independent tests. Verified: conditioned on gap size, CS's recovery is above the field median at 8 of 9 programs, 65th–93rd percentile. Panel A's raw-count series, the Q2/Q3 interiors, the cloud and the six per-program positions are illustrative — the cuts we need are raw and binding missing-course means by quartile, and per-program baseline-gap and recovery pairs.

Where this deviates, and what it still needs

Deviations

Five beats, five figures — no merges. Beat 5 is one figure with two panels because the two objections are only credible together: rescaling the currency without also conditioning looks like special pleading.

Beat 2 keeps geography, but as points. Filled district polygons would over-claim precision where service areas span whole counties. Same ordinal ramp on both panels, so the coincidence is a shape match rather than a colour-matching exercise.

Beat 4 gains a second panel. The brief asked for CS's named courses; the field's wealth-proof blockers sit beside them because "these are fixable, those are not" is the actionable half of the mechanism.

Two style departures from the exemplars. The grey dashed annotation layer is proposal scaffolding, not house style — it strips out for the paper. And the Wong categorical palette is barely used: quartiles are ordinal, so the navy ramp carries almost every figure, with the CS blue reserved for the subject line itself.
