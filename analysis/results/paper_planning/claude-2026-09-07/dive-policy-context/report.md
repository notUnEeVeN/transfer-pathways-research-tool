## 5. Mechanism narratives

### Massachusetts
MassTransfer is a Board policy, not a statute, and it operates on two layers with different reach. The GE Foundation (34 credits; 28 inside STEM maps) is universal by policy: every public institution must recognise it, with a hard ceiling of six extra credits, which is why our GE-inclusive lens can legitimately treat GE as satisfied everywhere. Major preparation, by contrast, is negotiated discipline-by-discipline into A2B Mapped pathways, and each university opts in per major; for CS only four of eleven universities have ever mapped (Bridgewater, Fitchburg, UMass Amherst, UMass Dartmouth), and Greenfield's four maps have vanished since the paper's snapshot. Where a map exists the CS block moves as a block and at least 60 credits must be *applied* to the 120–128; where no map exists the student falls back to a course-by-course equivalency database that the state itself calls "subject to verification". So Figure 1's CC×university variance in MA is mostly the articulation layer — which pairs were ever mapped and which equivalencies were ever entered — and the orchestrator's 56.9% (mapped) vs 32.7% (unmapped) split is the policy boundary made visible. Figure 3's "credits that satisfy a BS requirement" is a requirement-mismatch measure, not a credit-rejection measure, on the 38 (now 34) mapped pairs, because policy guarantees those credits count toward the degree even as electives. MassEducate (2024) changes price, not articulation, and the 2025 Steering Committee is the first formal move toward common course numbering ("MAST") — MA is today where CA was before AB 1111.

### California
California has the most statewide *identity* infrastructure (ASSIST as official repository since 1985, C-ID descriptors, a single 34-unit Cal-GETC since fall 2025, AB 1111 numbers arriving 2025–27) and the least statewide *decision* power over major articulation: UCOP is explicit that major and course-to-course articulation is decided by each UC campus, and ASSIST simply records those bilateral decisions for 115 colleges × 9 campuses. That is why CA Figure 1 has the widest CC×university spread and the lowest computing coverage (11.5% campus mean, whole degree) — the articulation layer is genuinely bilateral. The AS-T guarantee (SB 1440) is a CSU instrument: admission with junior standing somewhere in the CSU, no more than 60 units to finish, but no campus or major guarantee; UC is absent from the statute, TAG covers six campuses, UC Transfer Pathways are roadmaps, and the AB 1291 pilot starts at UCLA in fall 2026 with majors that are not confirmed to include CS. GE portability is also not universal at UC: Berkeley Engineering and UCLA Samueli do not accept IGETC/Cal-GETC, so the GE-inclusive assumption over-credits engineering-housed CS programs. The 70-unit cap converts over-preparation into subject credit only, so part of CA's Figure 4 excess is a cap artefact rather than a coursework artefact. Common course numbering will not touch any CS or calculus course before 2027, so nothing in the 2025–26 CA reforms changes the computing bottleneck our Figure 2 shows.

### Virginia
Virginia standardised three things by statute in 2018 (HB 919/SB 631, Code §23.1-907/908): course identity (the VCCS Master Course File, one prefix/number/credit/description for all 23 colleges), GE portability (Passport 15/16 and UCGS 30–32 credits that "shall satisfy a lower division general education requirement at any public institution"), and the obligation of every public four-year to publish a pathway map for every degree program, all served through one mandated portal. Because the demand side (university-authored guides) and the supply side (VCCS course numbers) share a key, the articulation question has a near-deterministic answer, which is exactly why our catalog-view Figure 1 is almost flat (37.5–49.7 by university, 50.4 at 11 of 16 colleges) and Figure 3 is ~100% by construction: the guide *is* the pathway, and the state's own 2025 claim is that >60% now transfer with an associate degree versus 29% in 2010. What the Master Course File does not do is require any college to offer or schedule a course; it fixes identity, not supply — so the same computing courses that fail to articulate in CA and MA (CS2/data structures, computer organisation, discrete) reappear in VA as courses listed but unscheduled at small colleges (133 of 240 cells miss something in scheduled view). The guarantees also stop at the admission door: GAAs are admission-only ("not necessarily entitled to be admitted to a specific program"), campus-specific (UVA A&S 3.4 GPA, ≤60 credits applied; VT CS 3.2 with a named associate degree), and the guides are advisory maps, not articulation agreements. Hence VA's figures see the articulation layer solved, the credit-application layer largely invisible (guides do not state how much of the degree the 60 credits satisfy beyond the ceiling), and the offering layer as the residual bottleneck.

## 6. Figure interpretations a policy fact contradicts

1. **AS-T as a UC guarantee** — contradicted by C1/C2/C3. Say "CSU admission guarantee; UC only via the AB 1291 pilot from fall 2026 (majors unconfirmed for CS)".
2. **VA transfer guides as "agreements"** — contradicted by V3/V9. They are statutory pathway maps authored by the university; the agreement layer is the GAA, which guarantees admission only. VA Figure 3 ≈ 100% is therefore not evidence of superior credit application.
3. **MA Figure 3 as "credit loss"** on mapped pairs — contradicted by M5: on A2B pathways ≥60 credits must be applied within 120–128, so the 68% (final PDF, 61 pathways) measures named-requirement mismatch, not credits rejected.
4. **GE-inclusive lens as "policy-guaranteed" everywhere** — MA: true, with a ≤6-credit ceiling (28-credit STEM block with ≤12 extra); VA: true by statute for Passport/UCGS; CA: not true for UC engineering-housed CS (C9). State the ceilings.
5. **Treating MassTransfer as universal** — M7/M8/M10: CS maps exist at 4 of 11 universities and 10 of 15 CCs; the map set is drifting (Greenfield lost 4 pairs), so any MA number must name its vintage.
6. **"VA standardised its AS requirements with every school"** — partly supported: common curricula for 28 disciplines and standardised associate requirements are a Transfer VA claim (V7, CONTEXT), but the Master Course File standardises identity only (V6); offering is local, which our scheduled-supply view confirms.

## 8. Comparisons the policy facts make defensible

1. **Articulation regime → Figure 1 dispersion**: CA bilateral campus decisions (widest CC spread), MA discipline-level opt-in maps (bimodal: mapped vs unmapped pairs), VA statutory per-program maps on a shared course key (flat). Report dispersion per university, not just means.
2. **Where the bottleneck lives**: CA/MA articulation layer vs VA offering layer — defensible because VA's Master Course File fixes identity but not supply (V6) and our catalog-vs-scheduled views isolate exactly that.
3. **GE portability as a controlled block**: MA 34 (+≤6) / 28 STEM (+≤12), CA 34 Cal-GETC (not at UC engineering units), VA 30–32 UCGS — comparable in size, so GE-inclusive vs GE-excluded deltas across states are interpretable as "how much of the degree the state chose to standardise".
4. **Admission guarantee vs credit-application guarantee**: MA A2B guarantees both admission (2.5) and ≥60 credits applied; CSU guarantees admission and ≤60 to finish; UC guarantees neither (cap 70); VA GAAs guarantee admission only. This is the axis on which Figures 3/4 must be read, and it is fully documented.
5. **Coverage of the guarantee population**: MA maps reach 4 of 11 universities for CS; CA AS-T reaches CSU only (0 of 9 UCs in our corpus); VA maps reach all 15 universities by statute — a policy-reach table the paper can put beside the figures.
6. **Vintage drift as a finding**: MA lost Greenfield's four CS maps between the paper's snapshot and 2026-09-07; CA moved IGETC→Cal-GETC in fall 2025; VA guides are re-posted each catalog year — all three corpora must be dated.
