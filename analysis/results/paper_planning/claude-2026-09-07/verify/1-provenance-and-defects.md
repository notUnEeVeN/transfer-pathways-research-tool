## Claim under review

"Ceiling-normalisation reorders the states: VA reaches 99.5% of its structural ceiling
(catalog), CA cs 68.2%, and MA only 66.4% (57.5% on lower-division columns alone), so
Massachusetts, not California, articulates the smallest share of what its community
colleges could teach."

Note: the originating dossier directory `scratchpad/dossier-fig1/` contains scripts and
outputs (`dist_out.txt`, `ma_out.txt`, `va_out.txt`, `ma_units_out.txt`) but NO `report.md`;
the write-up existed only in that agent's structured output. I audited from its scripts and
raw outputs.

## Verdict: REFUTED AS STATED (materially misleading), with a survivable corrected form

The three percentages are three different constructs, one of the three depends on an
uncommitted regenerated artefact, and the headline CA-vs-MA gap (1.8 points) does not by
itself establish "smallest"; only the parenthetical like-for-like figure (MA 57.5%) does.

### 4. Like-for-like: the three ratios are three constructs (the substantive problem)

| state | numerator | "ceiling" denominator | lens | can exceed 100? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CA cs | named slots articulated (nontransferable-tier slots never count) | named slots not stamped nontransferable (template author) | GE-excluded course counts | no (max 100.0; the 100.1 is 1-dp rounding) |
| MA | ALL articulated heatmap columns, upper-division included (paper marks "Upper Level Elective (3000)" etc. as articulated: 192 of 1582 articulated cells are upper columns) | lower-division columns (paper's Lower column) | GE-excluded course counts | **yes: 21 of 165 cells > 100, Fitchburg university mean 134%** |
| VA | supplied pre-transfer guide units + assumed GE | the guide's own pre-transfer list (`va_ceiling_pct`) | GE-INCLUDED units (the other two lenses give 99.4/99.4) | no; ≈ 1 − missing-supply share by construction |

- The MA 66.4 is therefore not a share of a ceiling; it is a mean of ratios that includes
  values above 1. Capping each cell at 100 gives 62.7; using the like-for-like numerator
  (lower-division columns only) gives 57.5 (VERIFIED from `ma_cells.json`).
- The CA ratio is identical to lower-division-only coverage by construction (dossier's own
  caveat). So the like-for-like pair is CA 68.2 vs MA 57.5, not 68.2 vs 66.4.
- Indicative precision (cells treated as independent, which they are not — 9 UCs vs 11
  universities): CA − MA(uncapped) = 1.8 pts, SE ≈ 2.7; CA − MA(capped) = 5.5, SE ≈ 2.1;
  CA − MA(lower-only) = 10.7, SE ≈ 2.0. The headline pair cannot support "smallest";
  the parenthetical pair can.
- VA: the VA audit doc (working tree) says Fig 1 "subtracts missing supply from the stated
  pre-transfer half … should be described as modeled guide preparation availability … It
  should not be called a literal reproduction of the Massachusetts paper's named-course
  measure." A university-authored guide lists what to take at the CC, so coverage ÷ that
  list is ~1 unless the CC lacks the course. "VA reaches 99.5% of its structural ceiling"
  is a course-supply statement about a self-referential denominator, not an articulation
  share of "what CCs could teach" (the university-only 46.5% of the VA denominator may
  contain lower-division work the guide simply did not list).

### 5. Would it survive the other Codex audit (docs/analysis-audit-2026-09-07.md)?

The numbers survive; the wording does not. The audit leaves CA course-count numerators
unchanged, preserves the MA raw archive, and regenerated VA (99.5 IS the post-audit
figure). But the audit attaches `method_status: estimated` and source warnings to every
VA Fig 1 cell, labels VA Fig 1 "modeled guide preparation availability", flags that the
stored MA database is behind the committed raw (served 66.2 ≠ claimed 66.4), and its
whole framing is that different constructs must not be equated ("Contracts distinguish
guide supply, estimated courses …"). A sentence equating a VA guide-supply ratio, a
CA capped tier ratio and an MA uncapped ratio as one "share of structural ceiling" is
exactly what that audit was written to prevent.
